# Benchmarks rerun on a rented RTX 4090 with a Ryzen 9 9950X3D

> Before release 0.4.0 the maintainer asked for every published benchmark to be measured again on a newer machine, an RTX 4090 with a competent CPU, rented on Lium.
> One secure-tier pod with a 16-core Ryzen 9 9950X3D ran all five reference engines and every Henad row at `885a0d2`, the merge of #54, following the runbook of [#48](20261009-48-rtx-4090-rerun.md).
> Its single-thread sysbench score is 3.4 times that of #48's Ryzen 9 3900.
> All 24 port gates passed, the 208-run sweep finished in 6.1 hours, and Henad's own 424-configuration matrix in 6.1 more.
> Henad on one thread still leads every reference engine at every rung, and eleven rungs changed order among the others or between Henad's GPU row and them.
> No runbook step broke, and two of #48's fixes were not needed on this host.

### Validating the CPU

A 62-second `mpstat` ran beside `sysbench cpu`, at one thread and at 32, twice each, on each of the two pods.
Steal time was 0.00% in every sample.

| | Ryzen 9 3900 (#48) | Ryzen 9 9950X3D |
|---|---|---|
| sysbench, one thread | 2,088 and 2,052 events a second | 7,039 and 7,020 |
| sysbench, every thread | 26,244 and 26,231 on 24 | 109,692 and 109,550 on 32 |

The container's `cpu.max` allows 31 CPUs, one fewer than `nproc` reports.
Rust's `available_parallelism` reads that quota, so `henad-cli --info` reports 31 logical CPUs and 31 worker threads, and Henad's all-cores rows ran on 31 threads.
Every reference engine's timed variant runs on one thread, since `compare_bench.py` passes `--threads 1` and starts Julia with `--threads 1`.
The JVM sees 32 processors, which sizes only its collector and compiler threads.

### The sweep

`compare_bench.py --dry-run` planned 208 runs, and `--smoke` passed 32 of 32 in 52 seconds.
The full sweep ran in tmux from 16:12 to 22:20 UTC, with nothing else on the pod.

| | Ryzen 9 3900, `3c7c7e0` | Ryzen 9 9950X3D, `885a0d2` |
|---|---|---|
| ok | 178 | 184 |
| over budget | 8 | 10 |
| timeout | 4 | 2 |
| skipped after a slower rung | 18 | 12 |
| summed wall time | 6.58 h | 6.13 h |

Every ladder that stopped early climbs at least as far as before.
Six climb one rung further: Mesa to 2048² Game of Life, 4096² SIR and 10k boids, NetLogo to 4096² SIR, Agents.jl to 300k boids, and krABMaga to a million boids.
NetLogo's SIR and Agents.jl's boids time out on their new rung.
Four rungs that timed out with no reps now record two: Henad's one-thread boids at a million agents, NetLogo's boids at 10k and ants at 200k, and MASON's boids at 300k.
No row failed for any reason but the budget.

Henad's one-thread column runs 1.5 to 2.1 times as fast as on the 3900, and every reference engine 1.5 to 2.6 times as fast at most rungs.
krABMaga gained least, 1.45 to 1.5 times on boids.
NetLogo gained most at its small grids, 2.6 to 5.2 times from 64² to 256².
On the 3900 its 256² Game of Life and SIR cells took 9 and 7 times as long as its 128² cells for four times the patches, and here they take 4.7 and 5.2 times, so the 3900's cells are the odd ones, most likely a cache effect.
The all-cores column runs 31 threads where it ran 24, and gains 1.4 to 3.3 times, most at the large rungs, and 4.7 times at a million boids.
Henad's boids at a million agents on all cores fell from 108.6 seconds to 23.3, 4.7 times as fast, and on the 3900 that cell took 8.1 times the 300k cell for 3.3 times the agents, where here it takes 3.4 times.

The GPU column is the same card on an older driver, 580.126.20 against 595.58.03.
Boids from 3k up and the top two rungs of the other three models agree within 3%.
Game of Life up to 1024² and SIR up to 128² run 21 to 25% faster, where a step is mostly submission overhead and the CPU that submits it is faster.
Three small rungs run 8 to 12% slower, SIR at 256² and ants at 6k and 20k, steady across their reps.

### Rankings

Henad on one thread leads every reference engine at every rung, as before.
Eleven rungs changed order elsewhere, against the 8 October tables.

- **SIR at 64².**
  Henad's GPU row, 0.78 ms, now falls behind Agents.jl and MASON, 0.69 and 0.74 ms.
  A step there costs the GPU a fixed submission, and the CPU engines got faster.
  Henad's one-thread and all-cores rows now tie, 0.374 and 0.376 ms.
- **Boids from 1k to 100k.**
  MASON overtakes krABMaga at 1k, 10k, 30k and 100k, and already led at 3k, since krABMaga gained the least of any engine on boids.
- **Ants at 20k and 60k.**
  Mesa overtakes NetLogo, by 9% at both.
  At 60k MASON and krABMaga swap places on a 1% gap.
- **Game of Life at 64² and 512², SIR at 256² and 512².**
  Agents.jl, krABMaga and MASON swap neighbouring places, by 1 to 2% in Game of Life and by 14 to 31% in SIR, where Agents.jl gained 2.6 times at 512².

The page has no prose about the order of the reference engines, and none was added.

### Henad's own matrix

`bench_matrix.py` ran next, queued in tmux to start only if the sweep exited 0, from 22:20 to 04:26 UTC.
Its output is `results/9_bench_matrix_lium4090zen5_885a0d2e5b6af1d6da34cab81181d080a14db018.csv`, unpublished like the eight before it.

The 32 errors are again the 0×0 grid rung, and the six timeouts again `gpu_boids` at a million agents over 100,000 steps.
The matrix runs every CPU model on all cores, 31 threads here and 24 there.
At the longest and most warmed configuration of each size, CPU throughput is 1.3 to 3.1 times #48's: Game of Life 1.5 to 2.6 times, SIR 1.4 to 3.1, boids 1.9 to 2.9, ants 1.5 to 2.8 and `virus_network` 1.3 to 2.6.
`virus_network` now peaks at 1.7e9 node updates a second at a million nodes, where #48 measured 8.9e8 at 100k and 6.7e8 at a million.

The GPU rows ran on a different card of the same model, on an older driver, 580.126.20 against 595.58.03.
At 1,000 steps without warm-up the two agree within 3% at the largest sizes.
Over longer runs #48's machine got faster, reaching 126,000 steps a second on Game of Life at 4096² over 100,000 steps, and this one stayed near 91,000, 28% lower.
Ants at 10k lost the same 28%, and Game of Life at 8192² and SIR at 1024² lost 10%.
Every one of those cells costs 10 to 25 µs a step.
The runs where a step costs about 0.3 ms or more agree within 1%: SIR at 4096² and 8192² and ants at a million agents over 100,000 steps, and boids at 100k over 100,000 steps and at a million over 1,000.
A power or clock limit on the card would slow the heavy runs first, and they match, so the gap sits in the fixed cost of each light step, which points at the driver or the host rather than the card.
The cause was not checked before the pod went.
The sweep's GPU cells time 100 steps after a short ramp, never reach that regime, and agree with #48's.

## Issues found & future directions

- **NetLogo's fixture export still stamps the time.**
  The gates rewrote the same two boids fixtures as in #48 with nothing but a new timestamp.
- **Lium's node record can disagree with the pod.**
  It listed this GPU at 315 W, and the pod reported 450 W.
  Check `nvidia-smi` on the pod rather than the listing.
- **A container quota one below the thread count.**
  This host's quota was 31 against 32 threads.
  Henad follows the quota, since `available_parallelism` reads it, and an engine that sized its pool from `nproc` would run 32 threads under a 31-CPU quota and be throttled in bursts.
- **The SSH key lives in 1Password.**
  It is the only key registered with Lium, and SSH failed until the maintainer approved its use there.
  After that one held master connection carried the run, and a reconnection after the master dropped needed no new approval.
- **The matrix's light GPU runs came out up to 28% slower than #48's.**
  Only cells costing 10 to 25 µs a step lost ground, and the heavy runs match, which points at the per-step cost of the driver or host rather than the card.
  Nothing on the pod was logged that would settle it.
  Logging `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=clocks.sm,power.draw,temperature.gpu` beside the matrix, and timing an empty dispatch on both drivers, would.
- **`bench_matrix.py` still asks for a 0×0 grid**, as #48 found.
- **The matrix history now spans three CPUs.**
  `plot_bench_history.py` plots every commit on one axis, so the Threadripper, the 3900 and the 9950X3D read as though only the commit changed.
